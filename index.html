The core idea

Recurring spending already exists. The opportunity is to turn it into an economic asset we can manage together.

The demand already exists in everyday business purchases. Coordinating compatible orders can reduce duplicated work and create value through volume and better execution.

Turn recurring purchases into a coordinated economic resource.
Why here, and why now?

Chicago already has the scale. The next step is to convert that scale into organized purchasing power.

Chicago’s logistics market connects businesses that repeatedly buy parts, equipment and supplies. The opportunity is to identify compatible demand and test whether it can be served more efficiently together.

The first 101 days

The first goal is not to look large. It is to prove that the model works.

Measure real recurring demand first. Then test it through real purchasing. Scale only what produces a measurable result.

1

Demand study

Identify and quantify the products and services participants buy repeatedly.

2

Founding group

Define the people, capital, responsibilities and rules before committing to scale.

3

Live pilot

50–100 SKUs. Real orders. Real costs. Measurable results.

4

Formal operating launch

Turn only the model proven by the pilot into a formal operating system.

What should we leave behind?

The strongest result is not one year of profit. It is a system that continues creating value without depending on its founders.

The goal is to leave more than capital: working rules, infrastructure, relationships and accumulated know-how so the next generation does not have to start from zero.

We are not building permanent jobs for ourselves. We are building a foundation others can continue from.
Before deciding

Three questions before you decide.

There is no automatic “yes.” A thoughtful “no” or “not yet” can be just as valid. The decision should follow from facts, values, and your time horizon.

Economics

After reviewing the numbers, do I believe the economics of pooled purchasing are credible?

People and rules

Do I trust the people and governance framework enough to build something together?

Time horizon

Do I want to help build an institution designed for more than a single transaction?

The right answer may be “yes,” “no,” or “not yet.” What matters is that the decision is based on evidence rather than impression.
